Course Description and Prerequisites

(Prereq: Senior standing in Applied Computer Science, CSCI A220 with a grade of C or better, and CSCI A360 with a grade of C or better)Each student is required to do an in-depth study of some topic or a team-based software design project in a student's final year of study. Lecture topics include project identification, project planning, software requirements analysis, design, specification, ethics, and social impact. Selection, specification, and feasibility study of an open-ended design project are to be completed.

Course Objectives

Upon completion of this course and CSCI592,

  • Students will be able to identify, plan, and manage a technical project from conception to completion, including a feasibility study.
  • Students will learn to apply principles of the software development lifecycle, including requirements analysis, design, and specification.
  • Students will function effectively on a team, manage project tasks, and collaborate to achieve a common goal.
  • Students will produce clear and comprehensive technical documentation, write professional emails, deliver clear and well-organized presentation, and demonstrate effective interpersonal communication skills among the teams.

Grading Policy

GRADING SCALE:

  • 90-100 A, 87-89 B+
  • 80-86 B, 75-79 C+
  • 68-74 C, 65-67 D+
  • 60-64 D, 0-59 F

Participation(10%): Students are required to attend all group meetings and team meetings with an industry/faculty mentor, and submit written/oral feedback as required.

Weekly report/stand-up meeting(20%): Students will be evaluated based on their assigned task completion and presentation(individual scores). Teams are expected to update their progress weekly in project management tools and each team member will give a short presentation in weekly stand-up meeting.

Project proposal and review(10%): Each team is required to submit a project proposal, which includes the problem statement, feasibility analysis, project roadmap, tech stack, and so on.

Project grade(25%): This grade is determined by the project progress, completion of project objectives, project management, and team effectiveness.

Individual performance(25%): The grade is based on each student's personal design and implementation contribution, peer review, writing and oral communication assignments, and ethical module.

Final presentation and report(10%): Each team will give a final presentation on the latest product release, issues and technical limitations, and relevant industry standards.

  10. Use of Generative AI Tools in this Course

    The University of South Carolina Aiken is committed to preparing students for their future roles as citizens, scholars, and professionals while recognizing the potentially positive impact artificial intelligence tools have on fostering academic, professional, and creative pursuits. As AI becomes increasingly embedded across industries and career fields, students are encouraged to develop skills that foster responsible and ethical use of AI tools when appropriate. Responsible use of AI includes adhering to principles of academic integrity, properly acknowledging the use of AI when required, and ensuring that submitted work reflects the student’s own understanding and contributions. Students should also be mindful of data privacy and security and should not share sensitive, personal, or confidential information when using AI tools. Because learning goals and assignments vary across disciplines and classes, course faculty determines whether AI is permitted and under what conditions. Students should follow faculty guidelines and seek clarification from course faculty if needed. For Writing Intensive (WI) courses, guidelines may differ than other classes. Students remain responsible for adhering to the University of South Carolina Aiken’s Code of Conduct and expectations related to academic integrity.

    Permitted and Encouraged Use:

    • Learning and Tutoring: You may use AI to understand concepts, explain code, and practice coding.
    • Large Projects: For large projects, you are encouraged to use tools like GitHub Copilot for coding and debugging.
    • Attribution: If you use AI tools in an assignment, proper documentation and citation are required.

    Prohibited Use & Strict Capstone Writing Policies:

    • Simple Programming Assignments and Exercises: You may not use AI tools to complete simple programming assignments and in-class exercises.
    • Discussion Forum: You may not use AI tools to generate discussion prompts.
    • Project Reports and Proposals: All course writing must be done on a shared drive where the document's revision history is continuously tracked and visible. AI tools may only be used for basic proofreading and grammar checks. You may not use AI to generate any single paragraph in your proposal or report. Points will be deducted for submitted writing that lacks a clear revision history or shows a generic, AI-like writing.
